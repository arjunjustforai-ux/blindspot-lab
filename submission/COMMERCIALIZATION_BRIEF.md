# Blindspot Lab: commercialization brief

*One page. Every market statement below is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.*

**Initial customer.** Marketing and business-analytics educators, business-school programmes and analytics-training providers: courses where learners routinely apply formulas to the wrong quantity (revenue vs contribution, ROAS vs profitability, correlation vs causation) and where instructors teach large cohorts.

**Buyer problem.** Quiz and LMS scores show *that* learners struggle, not *why* they got something wrong or whether they can apply a concept to a new situation. Instructors find out at the exam. Generic AI tutors answer from outside the course and cannot show where a claim came from, which makes them hard to approve for coursework.

**What we would sell.** A course companion that the instructor's own materials power:
* source-cited tutoring that declines off-material questions;
* verified, non-repeating assessments tagged to concepts and sources;
* misconception investigations that separate slips from misunderstandings, repair them with cited explanations, and check transfer on a different problem;
* an instructor view of which misconceptions are active in a cohort, with the evidence behind each.

**Differentiation.** Diagnosis and transfer-checked repair rather than explanation alone; provenance to the page, slide and lecture sentence; inspectable learner evidence; runs on local models, which matters to institutions that cannot send course material or student data to third parties.

**Possible revenue approach (to be validated).** A limited, low-cost institutional pilot on one course, followed by per-cohort or per-learner licensing per term if the pilot meets agreed success criteria. Instructor tooling (catalogue editing, cohort reports) and LMS integration would be part of the paid tier. No pricing has been tested.

**Potential moat.** Not the use of an LLM. The defensible assets would be instructor-reviewed misconception libraries for specific disciplines, assessment items with validated keys and difficulty, accumulated (consented, anonymised) learning evidence that improves parameter estimates, and integrations into the tools courses already use.

**Validation plan.**
1. Interview 10–15 instructors and programme leads: is "why did they get it wrong" a problem they would pay to solve, and who holds the budget?
2. Diagnosis agreement: have instructors label a sample of real wrong answers and compare with the engine's classifications and hypotheses.
3. Learning effect: in one course, compare transfer-test results for students using the Blindspot loop against a quiz-only group.
4. Teaching value: check whether cohort misconception reports change what instructors do in class.
5. Only then: fit BKT parameters to real data, extend the misconception catalogue to a second course, and test pricing.

**Key risks.** Diagnosis may not agree with instructors often enough to be trusted; building catalogues may be too costly per course; institutions may require LMS and data-protection certifications before any pilot; incumbent LMS vendors may add similar features.

**Current status.** Working prototype with one synthetic demo course and simulated-learner evaluation. No customers, pilots, partners or revenue.
